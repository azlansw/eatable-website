2. Results can be wrong

Eatable's answers come from sources that can be incomplete, out of date, or mistaken:

  • Barcode scans use Open Food Facts, a free database edited by volunteers. Products change their recipes, and listings can be missing or wrong.
  • Menu checks use AI (Google Gemini) and web search to guess what's in a restaurant's dishes. AI can misread menus, miss ingredients, and miss cross-contact in the kitchen. "Likely OK" is a guess, not a guarantee.
  • Trigger insights look for patterns in what you log. A pattern is not proof that a food causes your symptoms.
  • Restaurant maps use OpenStreetMap data, which may list places that have moved or closed.

A "LOOKS GOOD." result never means a food is safe for you. A "NOPE." result is a reason to check, not a diagnosis.
