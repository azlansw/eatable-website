The problem

Every meal is a guessing game.

For people with food allergies and conditions like EoE, eating takes research: reading every label, questioning every server, and trying to remember what you ate two days before a flare-up. The information is scattered, and the people cooking for you usually don't have it.

Labels are hard to read

Allergens hide under names like whey, casein and “natural flavors.”

Menus don't tell you

Most restaurants list dishes, not what's in them or what shares a fryer.

Reactions come late

Some show up hours or days after eating, so the cause is easy to miss.

Scan groceries

Scan it. Get a straight answer.

Scan a barcode and Eatable reads the ingredient list against your allergies and conditions, then answers in one word. It catches hidden names (whey = dairy) and “may contain” warnings, and skips false alarms like peanut butter on a dairy check.

  • 3M+ products from Open Food Facts
  • Explains every reason underneath
Eat out

Know what to order before you sit down.

Find restaurants on the map, tap one, and Eatable's AI looks up its real menu online, rates each dish for you, and links the source. Snap a photo of a paper menu and it reads that too.

  • Tells you what to ask the server
  • Check a menu for a friend, using their allergies
Find triggers

Find the food behind the reaction.

Log what you eat and how you feel. Eatable compares every food against your normal reaction rate, accounting for reactions that show up up to two days later, and flags the ones that stand out, like dairy coming before reactions 2.2× more often than usual.

  • Built-in statistics, explained by AI in plain words
  • Questions to bring to your doctor, never a diagnosis
Allergy card

One card for everyone who feeds you.

Your allergies, what to do in an emergency, who to call, and the foods that are safe to order for you. Text it as a link anyone can open, or show the QR code. With an account, it stays up to date automatically.

  • For servers, coaches, teachers and friends' parents
  • Your food logs stay on your phone
